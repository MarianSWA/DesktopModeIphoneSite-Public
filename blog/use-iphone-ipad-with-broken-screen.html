Broken iPhone or iPad Screen? Give It a Second Life on a Monitor

A green line down an iPhone screen. A black strip across an old iPad. A display that flickers, has dead touch, or stays black while the phone still rings. If the device still powers on and can output video, it does not have to become drawer clutter. Connect a monitor and XeOS gives it a usable desktop, often with more room to work than it had when its screen was perfect.

First: repair problem or reuse opportunity?

A cracked display, lines, blotches and failed touch are reasons to get a daily phone repaired. Back up important data first, and get service advice if the device is swollen, gets unusually hot, smells odd, or has been badly wet. Do not keep charging or using a damaged battery.

But an older spare device is different. If it turns on and can send a picture to a TV or monitor, a broken panel does not stop it being useful. It does not even need to run for long on battery: in a permanent desk or TV setup it can stay on power all the time. That makes a tired old phone with battery health well below 70% useful again, provided its battery is physically safe. The external screen is not a repair for the internal display; it is a way to keep a working computer, media player or display device in service.

The most common display issues

A failed display does not always look like a shattered screen. These are the problems that most often make an otherwise working iPhone or iPad difficult to keep using, and where moving the workspace to a monitor can help.

One restart is worth trying for a sudden software-looking glitch. Apple’s support steps cover force-restarting an iPhone or iPad whose screen is black or unresponsive. Persistent lines, blotches and dead areas after a drop or pressure are commonly physical display faults, so do not count on a reset to make them disappear.

What you need to prove the device still has a life

  1. Power it on and give it time. Listen for alerts, feel for vibration, or call it from another phone. If it appears dead rather than merely screen-blind, deal with that problem first.
  2. Connect a display and power at the same time. The best one-cable setup is a USB-C monitor that supplies power to the phone. Otherwise use a dock or hub with both video output and a USB-C power-input port, then connect a charger to the hub. Lightning iPhones need a Digital AV Adapter with its Lightning power port connected. That way a weak battery is not a limitation: the device stays powered while it is in use.
  3. Unlock it once. A screen that is fully black may still need its passcode after a restart. If touch works at all, enter it carefully; if it does not, connect a keyboard before restarting whenever possible.
  4. Pair a mouse and keyboard. They turn an awkward rescue setup into something you can actually use.
  5. Open XeOS. It takes over the external display and makes the monitor the desktop rather than a giant copy of the damaged phone screen.

Important: if the device was restarted and you cannot unlock it, no external-display app can bypass the passcode. Get it unlocked first, then use the monitor setup for recovery or reuse.

Why XeOS is the useful part, not just a bigger mirror

Stock iOS mirroring carries every limitation of the phone screen onto the monitor: portrait shape, black side bars, and controls built for a hand. That is particularly frustrating when the screen you are escaping is already unreliable.

XeOS: External Display Browser draws a full landscape desktop at the monitor’s own resolution. The phone can stay face down or become a trackpad; the external display gets resizable windows, a desktop browser, a file manager and a proper pointer. A cheap damaged iPhone becomes a small desktop machine instead of an expensive paperweight.

Use it like a desktop: mouse, keyboard and a real screen

This does not have to be a setup where you keep reaching for a broken phone. Pair a Bluetooth mouse and keyboard once, or plug wired USB versions into the same dock or hub that feeds the monitor. The mouse gives you a normal pointer for windows, buttons and scrolling; the keyboard handles typing, shortcuts and search. On a USB-C display with a built-in hub, they can often connect directly to the display.

With XeOS, the external monitor is where you see the desktop and work. You can move and resize windows, open several tabs, manage files, write in Google Docs and watch video full screen. If the phone’s touch still works, it can also act as a trackpad, but it is optional once a mouse is connected. Common questions

Can I use an iPhone with a completely black screen on a monitor?

Possibly, if the phone itself still runs and you can unlock it. The external screen may show the phone’s output, but it cannot bypass the passcode required after a restart.

Will connecting a monitor fix the green line or black bar on my phone?

No. It does not repair the built-in panel. It gives you another screen to use, which is often enough for a spare device or to move your data safely.

Does an old iPad need Stage Manager for this?

No. XeOS renders its own desktop on the external display, so it is useful on iPads that only mirror with Apple’s built-in display support.

Can I use the normal iPhone apps on the monitor?

iOS does not let one app put every other iPhone app into an external desktop. XeOS provides its own desktop tools plus the web, files, PDFs and installed web apps.
