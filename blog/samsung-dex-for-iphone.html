Samsung DeX for iPhone: What Actually Exists in 2026

Galaxy owners plug into a monitor and get a desktop. iPhone owners plug in and get their phone screen, enlarged, with black bars on both sides. Apple has never shipped its own version of DeX, but you can get most of the way there today. Here is what is possible, what isn't, and how to set it up.

October 17, 2026 · 4 min read

The short answer

iOS has no desktop mode. Connect any iPhone to a display and the system mirrors the phone: one app, portrait, touch only. There is no setting that changes this, on any model.

What you can do is use an app that draws its own desktop on the external screen. XeOS does exactly that. It renders a full environment for the display at its native resolution, with resizable windows, a dock, a file manager and a desktop browser, and the phone becomes the trackpad. It is the closest thing to DeX an iPhone can run.

Why iOS mirrors instead

When iOS sends a picture to a monitor, it sends the same picture the phone is showing. The phone's screen is tall and narrow, about 19.5:9, while monitors and TVs are 16:9, so the image is scaled to fit the height and the rest is left black. Ordinary apps can't ask for a landscape layout of their own on the second screen, which is why Safari, Mail and everything else look the same on a 27-inch monitor as they do in your hand.

Apps can, however, draw something completely separate on an external display. Very few do, because it means building every part of the interface themselves. XeOS is built entirely around that.

The honest difference is the sixth row. DeX belongs to Android itself, so any Android app can open in a window. On iOS no app can run another app inside it. XeOS makes up for that with its own apps and the whole web: Google Docs and Sheets, Office on the web, Gmail, YouTube, Netflix and nearly everything else you would open on a laptop at a desk. Any of those sites can be installed to the dock and open in its own window, like an app.

What a day with it looks like

  • Work: a document in one window, email in another, a spreadsheet behind them. Switch with the dock or Control + Tab.
  • Files: open a PDF from iCloud Drive, annotate it, sign it, save a copy to a USB stick.
  • Downtime: YouTube or a streaming site full screen on the TV, or cloud gaming with a controller.
  • Finding things: Control + Space opens Smart Search for apps, files, calendar events and your clipboard history.

Setting it up in five minutes

  1. Connect the display. On iPhone 15 and newer, a USB-C to HDMI adapter or a USB-C cable straight into a USB-C monitor. On iPhone 14 and older, Apple's Lightning Digital AV Adapter. AirPlay to an Apple TV or AirPlay TV works too, without a cable.
  2. Open XeOS. It finds the display and puts its desktop on it, in landscape, at the panel's resolution.
  3. Pick your input. Pair a Bluetooth mouse and keyboard in iOS Settings, or simply use the phone's screen as a trackpad.
  4. Install your sites. Add the web apps you use every day to the dock, so they open as apps in their own windows.

If the picture shows a thin black frame on a TV, that is the TV's overscan rather than the phone. The fix is in our guide to black bars on an external display.

Questions people ask

Will Apple add DeX-style desktop mode to iOS?

Not so far. iOS 26 and iOS 27 changed a lot, but connecting an iPhone to a monitor still mirrors it. Split View arrived only on iPhone Duo, and only on its own screens.

Which iPhones work?

Any iPhone that can output video, from the Lightning models to the newest USB-C ones, plus iPhone Duo and every iPad, including those without Stage Manager.

Do I need a mouse?

No. The phone's screen works as a trackpad, and on iPhone you can also move the phone in the air to move the pointer. A Bluetooth mouse makes it feel most like a computer.

Can I use it on a TV?

Yes: monitors, TVs, projectors, portable displays and even AR glasses like XREAL and VITURE.
